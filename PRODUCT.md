# Product

## Register

product

## Users

quadGEN serves photographers, fine-art printmakers, and print-studio technicians who build, inspect, and refine QuadToneRIP curves. They work from measured print data and existing curve files, often in iterative calibration sessions where small tonal changes must remain understandable, reversible, and exportable.

Their primary job is to turn LAB, CGATS, manual L*, LUT, ACV, or `.quad` inputs into dependable printer curves while preserving enough control to diagnose difficult inks, processes, and tonal transitions.

## Product Purpose

quadGEN makes high-precision QuadToneRIP calibration approachable without hiding the underlying curve behavior. It brings measurement ingestion, correction methods, curve editing, comparison, undo, and export into one browser-based workspace that can also ship as a self-contained offline file.

Success means an operator can understand the current curve state, make a deliberate adjustment, see its effect immediately, and export the intended result with confidence. Smooth transitions use PCHIP wherever interpolation is required.

## Brand Personality

Precise, calm, expert.

The product should feel like a trusted senior lab technician at a fine-art print studio: technically rigorous, patient, direct, and comfortable explaining advanced controls without talking down to the operator. Its emotional goal is quiet confidence during careful, consequential work.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS gloss: promotional dashboards, hero metrics, decorative gradients, glass effects, and visual noise that competes with the curve.
- Consumer photo-filter apps that reduce calibration to presets or hide meaningful processing choices.
- Novelty laboratory interfaces that imitate scientific instruments with dense chrome, tiny labels, or theatrical gauges.
- Inconsistent custom controls that replace familiar browser affordances without improving the workflow.

## Design Principles

1. **Keep the curve primary.** Controls, help, and status should make the print-calibration task easier to read, never compete with its chart and data.
2. **Expose consequential choices.** Processing methods and persistent preferences must be visible, clearly named, and explained close to the control.
3. **Earn expert confidence.** Use exact terminology, predictable state, reversible actions, and immediate feedback. Never imply precision the system cannot deliver.
4. **Teach in place.** Make advanced workflows approachable with concise labels, tooltips, and contextual guidance instead of promotional copy or unnecessary onboarding.
5. **Preserve familiar operation.** Reuse consistent buttons, fields, tabs, and dialogs so the interface disappears into the work.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA for user-facing controls and content. Preserve semantic HTML, complete keyboard operation, visible focus, sufficient text and control contrast, and useful accessible names. Never communicate channel, warning, selection, or correction state through color alone. Respect reduced-motion preferences and keep state transitions functional without animation.
